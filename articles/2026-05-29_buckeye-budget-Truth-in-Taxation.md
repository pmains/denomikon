The Buckeye City Council will hold a Truth-in-Taxation hearing and vote on adopting the city's final budget at a special meeting on June 2, a comprehensive agenda that sets the city's spending and tax policy for the next fiscal year.

The centerpiece of the meeting is a public hearing on the Truth-in-Taxation requirement, which Arizona law mandates when a city expects to collect more property tax revenue than the previous year, adjusted for new construction. The hearing requires a roll call vote. Any city that proposes a levy exceeding the statutory limit must hold a Truth-in-Taxation hearing and may face the possibility of voter protest.

Following the hearing, the council will convene a special council meeting to take action on two budget resolutions. Resolution 53-26 would adopt the city's final budget for the coming fiscal year. Resolution 28-26 would adopt a final element of the budget package — likely the property tax levy or the capital improvement plan, based on standard municipal budget procedures.

The council will also consider Ordinance 16-26, extending a city regulation or policy that expires at the end of the fiscal year.

Before the budget hearings, the council will act on several consent agenda items. These include a new Series 009 liquor license for a store, Resolution 46-26 enlarging the city's street lighting district, a change order on a job order contract, and Resolution 54-26 approving an intergovernmental agreement with another entity.

The same evening, the council is also scheduled to meet as the Buckeye Joint Community Facilities Districts to discuss the district's finances and operations. Community Facilities Districts are special taxing districts that finance infrastructure for new developments, common in fast-growing municipalities like Buckeye.

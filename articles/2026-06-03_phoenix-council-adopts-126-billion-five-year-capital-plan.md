A new library near Estrella Mountain. A bicycle and pedestrian bridge across the Rio Salado. Two light rail extensions. A police precinct at 31st Avenue and Thunderbird Road. And five new or replacement fire stations.

Those are among the nearly 1,000 projects in the $12.86 billion, five-year Capital Improvement Program the [Phoenix City Council will consider June 3](/meetings/phoenix-cc/City%20Council%20Formal%20Meeting-6%2F3%2F2026). The plan, adopted by [Resolution 22382](https://www.phoenix.gov/content/dam/phoenix/cityclerksite/city-council-meeting-files/2026/6-3-2026%20Formal%20AGENDA%20-FINAL.pdf), covers 26 program areas across the city's major infrastructure systems.

The Capital Improvement Program functions as a planning document that sets the city's priorities for major construction projects over five fiscal years, from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2031, without committing any money. Each project still needs annual budget approval before any funds can be spent. The first year of the program gets that approval when the council adopts the annual budget, which happens through a separate ordinance.

The council will also hold public hearings and vote on three tentative 2026-27 budget ordinances: one for operating funds, one for capital funds, and one for reappropriated funds. These together begin the formal process of setting the city's overall spending plan. The tentative budget will be advertised for two weeks before the council adopts a final version, scheduled for the June 17 formal meeting.

## Where the money goes

Five categories account for 83% of the five-year total, a concentration that reflects the capital-intensive nature of water, transportation, and airport infrastructure.

Water leads the plan at $2.88 billion over five years, the largest single category. Projects include the North Gateway Advanced Water Purification Plant, which would treat wastewater to drinking-water standards for reuse, and a host of pipeline, treatment plant, and reservoir upgrades across the city's water system.

Aviation is second at $2.84 billion, driven by Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport. Major projects include Terminal 3 North Concourse expansions, the Taxiway Uniform project (a $333 million taxiway currently under construction), and Deer Valley Airport runway reconstruction.

Wastewater accounts for $1.99 billion. The plan includes work on four treatment plants, sewer line systems, lift stations, and supporting facilities for infrastructure that handles the city's used water from homes and businesses.

Public Transit comes in at $1.56 billion, which includes the Capitol Light Rail Extension and the I-10 West Light Rail Extension. The council will also vote on a separate item authorizing up to $14.5 million to purchase fixed-route bus service from Valley Metro for fiscal year 2026-27, covering routes such as University Drive, Broadway Road, Southern Avenue, and Baseline Road that cross city boundaries.

Street Transportation and Drainage rounds out the top five at $1.37 billion, covering road widenings, drainage improvements, and safety upgrades. Specific projects include a bicycle and pedestrian bridge across the Rio Salado River at Third Street, improvements to Lower Buckeye Road from 27th Avenue to 19th Avenue, and 43rd Avenue improvements from Dove Valley Road to Carefree Highway.

## Parks, fire, police, and libraries

The plan allocates $384.7 million for Parks, Recreation and Mountain Preserves, including improvements at eight park facilities and new trails in the Phoenix Mountains Preserve system, such as the planned Sunset Mountain Preserve trails.

Fire Protection receives $157.5 million. The plan funds five new or replacement stations: Fire Station 51 near 51st Avenue and State Route 303; Fire Station 74 at 19th Avenue and Chandler Boulevard; and replacements for Fire Stations 7 (Fifth Street and Hatcher Road), 13 (48th Street and Thomas Road), and 15 (43rd Street and Highland Avenue). The council will also vote June 3 on architectural services for Fire Station No. 93, a new station in District 1.

Police Protection is budgeted at $53.9 million, including a new Cactus Park Precinct at 31st Avenue and Thunderbird Road.

Library spending totals $35.7 million. Two new libraries are in the works: the Estrella Civic Space Library at 99th Avenue and Lower Buckeye Road, and the Desert View Civic Space Library at Deer Valley Drive and Tatum Boulevard. Multiple library-related items are on the June 3 agenda, including professional services contracts for the Yucca Library Expansion in District 4 and the Sierra Estrella Library in District 7.

Housing receives $226.6 million. Human Services gets $14.8 million for projects including a new Cesar Chavez Senior Center, renovation of the McDowell Senior Center, and an Office of Homeless Solutions facility at the Interstate 17 and Northern Avenue area.

## Other items on the June 3 agenda

The council will also vote on several other significant items:

- **Briles Pass Annexation (Item 36)** — A proposal to extend Phoenix's corporate limits northward in District 1, requested by a property owner who wants the area to receive City of Phoenix services.

- **Valley Youth Theatre (Item 60)** — A construction manager at risk agreement with Chasse Building Team to build the Valley Youth Theatre, funded through general obligation bonds.

- **Sexual assault kits contract (Item 77)** — A five-year, $84,000 contract with Sirchie Acquisition Company for standardized sexual assault evidence collection kits used by the Phoenix Police Department.

- **Citywide contracts** — Items covering odor control, IT staffing, streetlight maintenance, firefighter equipment, and vehicle repair services, all on the consent agenda.

## What happens next

The tentative budget ordinances approved June 3 will be published for two consecutive weeks as required by state law. The council is scheduled to adopt the final 2026-27 budget on June 17, which will include the final operating budget, capital funds budget, and reappropriated funds budget ordinances. The property tax levy — set at a rate of $2.0397 per $100 of assessed valuation based on the council's May 19 action — will be formally adopted on July 1.

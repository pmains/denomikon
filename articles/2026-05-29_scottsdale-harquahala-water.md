The Scottsdale City Council will meet in executive session on June 9 to discuss purchasing water in the Harquahala Valley, a rural area in western Arizona that has become a focus of municipal water acquisitions.

The agenda item authorizes the council to discuss and consult with the city manager on the city's position regarding a water purchase. Council members will also consult with attorneys for legal advice on contracts related to the purchase. The discussion is closed to the public under Arizona law, which allows executive sessions for considering the city's position in purchasing real property and for legal advice on contracts.

The Harquahala Valley sits about 80 miles west of Phoenix, near the La Paz County line. The area sits above a large groundwater basin that several Valley cities have tapped in recent years as they plan for long-term water supply beyond their Colorado River allocations. The valley's groundwater, recharged by runoff from the Harquahala Mountains, has been the subject of multiple water-rights acquisitions by municipalities seeking to secure water for future growth.

Scottsdale currently uses a combination of Colorado River water, Central Arizona Project water, Salt River Project water, and local groundwater. The city's water management plan emphasizes supply diversification and has included groundwater storage programs. A purchase in the Harquahala Valley would represent an expansion of the city's water portfolio outside its immediate service area.

No action will be taken during the executive session itself. The council is also scheduled to hold a second special meeting the same day to consider nominees for five citizen boards and commissions.
